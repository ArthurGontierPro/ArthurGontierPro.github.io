About

Since January 2024 I am a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Glasgow, in the FATA section. I work with Ciaran McCreesh, Matthew McIlree and the VeriPB group on proof producing constraint solvers.

Before that I was a PhD student in symmetric cryptanalysis with declarative languages. I used several kinds of solvers — constraint programming, linear programming and Boolean satisfiability — as well as dedicated algorithms, to find differential characteristics or mount cube attacks, for example. I was working in the CAPSULE team in Rennes, supervised by Stéphanie Delaune, Patrick Derbez and Charles Prud'homme.

Earlier, I obtained a Master's degree in Optimisation and Operational Research in Nantes and Brussels and I did several research internships on multi-objective optimisation, static program analysis and CP explanations.

Research interests

  • Proof logging for constraint solvers. Making solvers justify every inference they make, so that their answers can be checked independently. My current work is on trimming VeriPB proofs to make verification tractable, and to explain the critical reasoning of solvers.
  • Generic solvers for cryptanalysis. Modelling cryptographic properties so that solvers can reason about them can lead to new discoveries, and if those solvers are certifying, we can derive security guarantees from their proofs.
  • Conflict analysis, explanations and proof logging. To make explanations and proof logging easier to use in CP solvers, I am interested in generating explanations for global constraints from their decompositions, and in externalising the justifications of propagators.
  • Generic cryptanalysis tooling. I contributed to TAGADA, a tool where anyone can model a block cipher and have differential cryptanalysis performed on it automatically. Such tools are valuable to automate and speed up cryptanalysis.
